01 · The challenge

Reporting existed. Decision clarity did not.

Large transformation programs generate abundant data, but the information is often distributed across tools, time windows and operating models. The real product problem was not creating another chart. It was creating a trustworthy layer between raw operational evidence and executive conversation.

What made the problem hard

  • Different capabilities measured different units of work.
  • Year-over-year periods were not always directly comparable.
  • Headcount alone did not explain productivity or workload.
  • Executive views needed simplicity without hiding methodology.
  • Local performance signals could be misread as causal conclusions.

The product question I used

How might we create one decision surface that helps leaders move from volume to productivity, from productivity to evidence, and from evidence to the right validation questions?

This reframing changed the dashboard from a reporting artifact into a diagnostic product. Every view needed to answer three things: what changed, what evidence supports the signal, and what should be investigated next.

02 · Product approach

A decision architecture, not a collection of charts.

I structured the solution as a sequence. Raw sources are governed first, then normalized into business rules, converted into comparable indicators, and finally translated into executive narratives and workstream questions.

01

Source governance

Clarify authoritative sources, remove duplicates, preserve provenance and identify missing fields.

02

Business rules

Define comparable windows, denominators, workforce scope and what each metric actually means.

03

Decision metrics

Prioritize productivity, workload, mix and confidence instead of presenting raw volume in isolation.

04

Executive dialogue

Translate signals into evidence, caveats, hypotheses and questions for validation with domain owners.

04 · Metric design

The metric is only useful if its meaning is explicit.

One of the highest-risk failure modes in executive dashboards is presenting precise-looking numbers without explaining their denominator, period or interpretation. I treated methodology as part of the UX rather than documentation hidden elsewhere.

PRODUCTIVITY

Normalize volume by capacity

Where meaningful, output is divided by the FTE population that actually contributes to that capability.

Productivity = comparable output / scoped FTE
COMPARABILITY

Align reporting windows

If one source covers fewer periods, the comparison uses the common closed window or clearly discloses the difference.

YoY delta = (current - baseline) / baseline
INTERPRETATION

Separate signal from cause

Throughput, backlog, workforce mix and workload can support a hypothesis. They do not prove why the result changed.

Evidence → hypothesis → owner validation → action
05 · Generative AI workflow

AI accelerated the build. It did not own the judgment.

I used generative AI as a development and analysis copilot across the product cycle. The differentiator was not prompt volume. It was the ability to connect business rules, UX, analytical reasoning and working front-end prototypes in rapid iterations.

Human-in-the-loop: business rules, source interpretation, causal restraint, stakeholder language and confidentiality decisions remained human-reviewed.

06 · What the solution demonstrates

Product thinking across data, UX and transformation.

I do not disclose confidential business outcomes in this public case. What I can demonstrate is the problem-solving model and the product capabilities designed to improve the quality of leadership conversations.

One decision surface

Performance, workload, capacity and evidence can be explored without forcing leaders to reconcile separate reports manually.

Methodology visible in the product

Definitions and caveats travel with the metric, reducing the risk of confident but incorrect comparisons.

Conversation over blame

Workstream diagnostics are framed as questions to validate, helping teams investigate drivers without presenting hypotheses as facts.

What I would evolve next

The next maturity step would move from a self-contained analytical product toward governed automated ingestion, role-based access, persistent scenario history and a formal metric catalog. I would preserve the same principle: automation should increase traceability, not remove human interpretation from ambiguous program signals.
